GP2040 Remap - easy button remapping for GP2040-CE controllers
================================================================

How to use
1. Unplug your controller.
2. Hold Start (S2) and plug it into your PC. It starts in setup mode.
3. Open "GP2040 Remap.exe". It finds the controller by itself.
4. Click a button on the picture, then press the real button you want it to be.
   If that button already did something, the two swap.
5. Press "Save to controller", then "Finish and use controller".

Find out what a button is (auto-detect)
- While nothing else is going on, the app listens for presses. Press any
  button on your controller and it shows its GPIO pin and what it does,
  and that button glows green on the picture.
- To change it, click what it should be on the picture, or pick from the
  list on the right (open "More functions" for extras). If the new job was
  on another button, the two swap.
- Unused buttons show as "doesn't do anything yet", so you can find them
  and give them a job.
- Turn it off with the "Auto-detect button presses" checkbox if you like.

Two buttons that do the same thing (e.g. two Up buttons)
- Tick "Let two buttons do the same thing" on the right (or pick
  "Keep both" when you're changing a button).
- Click Up on the picture and press your second Up button. Both GPIO pins
  are now Up, and a second Up appears on the picture showing its own GPIO.
  Drag it where it really is in Arrange picture.
- Or in Arrange picture, click a button and press "Add another ..." to put
  a copy on the picture, then click the copy and press its real button.
- To stop one of them being Up, click Up and press "Remove GPIO ...".
- With "Swap" (the default), giving a job to a new button moves it off the
  old one, and the old one takes the new button's previous job.

Make the picture look like your controller
- Press "Arrange picture" (above the picture).
- Drag buttons to where they are on your controller. Click one to make it
  bigger or smaller, or to take it off the picture. Add missing ones from
  the list on the right. On the fightstick picture, the lever can be moved
  too (hold Shift to move it without its four directions).
- Arrow keys nudge the selected button; Shift + arrow moves it further.
- Press "Done arranging". This only changes the drawing, never the controller.

Add more buttons
- In "Arrange picture", open "Add more buttons" on the right. You can add
  anything a GP2040-CE button can do:
  - Extra buttons: A3, A4, Extra 1-12
  - Second set of directions (dual directional input)
  - Mode switches: D-pad / left stick / right stick mode, SOCD modes,
    4-way / 8-way, Focus mode, Reverse input
  - Macros: Macro, Macro 1-6
- Then press "Done arranging", click the new button on the picture and
  press the real button you want for it. Press Save to controller.
- Macros and mode switches only do something if that feature is set up
  in GP2040-CE's own settings (for example, the macro itself).

My pads (save several controllers)
- Press "My pads" to open the gallery.
- "Save current pad" keeps the picture and the buttons shown right now
  under a name. "Start a new pad" begins from a fresh leverless or
  fightstick picture.
- Use: switch the picture to that pad.
- Load its buttons: put that pad's button setup on the plugged-in
  controller (then press Save to controller).
- "Update this pad's buttons" (above the picture) stores your latest
  buttons in the pad you're using.
- Rename, Duplicate and Delete are on each pad. Deleting a pad never
  changes your controller.

Share a pad with a friend
- In My pads, press "Share" on a pad. It saves a .gp2040pad file in
  Documents\GP2040 Remap pads and opens that folder.
- Send the file (Discord, USB stick, anything).
- Your friend opens My pads and presses "Import pad", or drops the file
  onto the gallery. They get your picture and button setup.
- Button setups use GPIO pin numbers, so they fit controllers with the
  same board and wiring. The app warns if the board is different.

Other things it can do
- Guided setup: press every button in order to set up a whole new layout.
- Test buttons: press buttons and watch them light up on the picture.
- Names: show buttons as Xbox, PlayStation, Switch, Arcade or GP2040 names.
- Backups: your old layout is saved automatically before the first save,
  in Documents\GP2040 Remap backups. You can load one back from the app.

Try it without a controller
Open "GP2040 Remap (demo).exe". It uses a pretend controller; click the
GPIO buttons under the picture to pretend you pressed a real button.

Notes
- Needs Windows 10 or 11.
- Windows may show a SmartScreen warning for a new download, even when
  it's signed. Click "More info" then "Run anyway".
- The app window opens through Microsoft Edge (built into Windows) in app
  mode, so it looks like its own program. If Edge isn't there it uses
  Chrome, or your normal browser.
- If an antivirus flags it, that's a false positive for a new program.
  You can report it to Microsoft at
  https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/wdsi/filesubmission (choose
  "Incorrectly detected as malware").
- If Windows still shows an old icon, that's its icon cache; it updates
  after moving the file or restarting Explorer.
- This changes Profile 1 (the main button layout). Add-on pins (LEDs,
  screens, etc.) are never touched.
- Built for current GP2040-CE firmware (October 2026). Very old firmware
  without the "press to assign" pin mapping feature may not work.
